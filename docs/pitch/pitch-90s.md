# MAPAY: 90-second pitch

**Target spoken length:** about 220 words. Screen directions are not spoken.

## 1. The commute — hook

**On screen:** Map centered on Miami; show the BBC → MMC route and the congestion, flood-risk and construction layers.

Leaving BBC at 5:10, you hit the Palmetto in red. On your way home, the group chat asks if the street by your apartment is underwater again. That’s Miami: same commute, different surprise. Mapay starts with the trip you already make, not a blank search box.

## 2. The heads-up

**On screen:** Show the FIU routine, tap **Fire heads-up now**, then show the notification or widget on the Lock Screen.

Our FIU routine goes MMC to BBC at 9:30, then back from BBC to MMC between five and seven. I tap “Fire heads-up now.” The lock-screen reminder tells me when to leave, what’s on the way, and gives me two choices: Start or Customize. Start opens the route in Google Maps.

## 3. Customize the route

**On screen:** Open Customize, enter the prompt, then show the old and new routes.

I want coffee, and I don’t want the Palmetto. I type, “Stop at a Starbucks and stay off the Palmetto.” Mapay turns that into route rules, then shows the old and new routes. Gemini understands the request; MAPAY’s routing rules pick the road.

## 4. The map’s data

**On screen:** Return to the map; tap a flood or construction feature to show its source and timestamp. Use a verified satellite pass if one is available.

Now the map explains why. Cyan is flood risk around known hotspots, informed by tide and flood-zone data; orange is construction from city records, with satellite evidence shown when a pass is available. Traffic, closures, weather and local-news incidents have their own marks. Tap one and you can see the source and when it was checked.

## 5. Close

**On screen:** Show the customized route and **Open in Google Maps**.

Mapay can’t make Miami traffic disappear, and a satellite doesn’t watch every block every minute. It gives commuters one clearer picture before leaving, then lets them choose: take the route or change it. Fewer surprises between campus and home—that’s the idea.
